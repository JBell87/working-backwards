# Example: Critique mode

**Prompt used:** "Can you review my PR/FAQ before I send it to my director?" followed by the document below.

**Reading taken:** Critique mode. Review only, no rewrite, no build.

## The PR/FAQ submitted (deliberately weak, written for this test)

> **Introducing TeamPulse AI: The Next-Generation Engagement Platform**
>
> SEATTLE - TeamPulse AI leverages cutting-edge large language models and a proprietary sentiment graph to empower organisations of all sizes to unlock best-in-class employee engagement. Managers, HR leaders, executives and employees can all benefit from real-time insights, automated pulse surveys, burnout prediction, recognition feeds and Slack integration.
>
> "We are thrilled to launch TeamPulse AI," said the CEO. "It has every feature our customers could want."
>
> "TeamPulse AI is a game changer for our organisation," said a customer.
>
> **FAQ**
> Q: Is it secure? A: Yes, it uses enterprise-grade security.
> Q: How much does it cost? A: Pricing is competitive.
> Q: Will customers like it? A: Yes, engagement is a $2B market growing 40% a year.

## Review

**This is not ready to send. Three problems threaten the idea itself, not just the writing.**

**1. There is no customer.** "Organisations of all sizes" and "managers, HR leaders, executives and employees can all benefit" name four different people with different problems. A manager wants to know who on their team is struggling; an employee may not want their manager to know. Until one is chosen, nothing else can be judged.
*Question to answer:* who is the one person who would go looking for this, and what are they doing the day before they find it?

**2. There is no problem.** The release goes from headline to features without saying what is wrong today. "Unlock best-in-class employee engagement" is an outcome nobody would say out loud. There is no problem paragraph at all, and no description of how the customer copes now.
*Question to answer:* what happened, concretely, the last time this customer suffered from not having it?

**3. Five features, no benefit.** "Real-time insights, automated pulse surveys, burnout prediction, recognition feeds and Slack integration" is a roadmap. The CEO quote, "it has every feature our customers could want", confirms no choice has been made. One of these is the product; the rest are distractions or later versions. Burnout prediction is the only one that is new, and it is also the one with the hardest questions.
*Question to answer:* if you could ship only one of the five, which, and why would the customer in (1) pay for that alone?

**Further issues**

- *Insider language in a customer document:* "large language models", "proprietary sentiment graph", "leverages", "empower", "next-generation". The customer cares what it does for them, not how.
- *The customer quote says nothing:* "a game changer for our organisation" could be about any product. A real quote names the before and after.
- *No launch date, no how-it-works, no call to action.* Without a date there is no view on scope.
- *The FAQ avoids every hard question.* "Enterprise-grade security" and "pricing is competitive" are not answers. "Will customers like it?" is answered with a market size, which is a different question, and the $2B and 40% figures have no source.
- *The questions the director will ask are missing:* Is predicting an individual's burnout from their messages lawful and acceptable to employees, and in which countries? What data does it read? What happens when the prediction is wrong and a manager acts on it? Why would a buyer choose this over the survey tool they already have? What would make you stop?

**What is worth keeping.** Burnout prediction for a specific manager is a real idea with a real, uncomfortable trade-off, which is a good sign: it is specific enough to be wrong.

**Suggested next step.** Answer the three questions above in a sentence each before rewriting anything. If the answers come easily, the rewrite will take an hour. If they do not, that is the finding.
